WHAT THIS LEVEL IS
───────────────────
Chapter 4 / Final chapter before the ending sequence.
The border between Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan.
A concrete wall running east-west across the map.
The northern half is Kazakhstan. The southern half is Uzbekistan.
Rustam approaches from the south.
The Kazakh guard patrols the wall on the northern side.
The TNT is in a supply depot on the Kazakh side.
The wall comes down here. The water follows.

TONE: Tense but not hostile. Both sides want the same thing.
The wall is a bureaucratic accident, not an act of war.
The level should feel like a conversation waiting to happen.

MAP SIZE: 36 tiles wide x 28 tiles tall = 1152 x 896 pixels


ZONE LAYOUT
────────────

ZONE A — Uzbek Side, Southern Approach (rows 0-11)
  Sandy desert floor — same tile palette as Seafloor
  Rustam enters from the north edge of Seafloor
  A dirt track leads north toward the wall
  Two Soviet-era warning signs along the track:
    Sign 1 tile (10,4): "[BORDER ZONE — Authorised Personnel Only]"
    Sign 2 tile (25,4): "[Keep to the marked path]"
  MINE FIELD — rows 6-9, scattered across map width
    3 mine instances (trap_mine.gd)
    Placed at: tile (8,7), tile (19,8), tile (27,6)
    Clear safe path exists between them at x=14-16
    The guard warns about these in first dialogue
  Small concrete observation post (decorative) tile (16,9)
    Rustam can shelter behind it
    Interact: "[Soviet concrete. Poured in 1965. Still standing.]"

THE WALL (rows 12-13) — full width of map
  Solid concrete wall tiles — full collision both rows
  Only breakable at center: tiles (15,12) to (20,12)
  These 6 tiles are NorthWall.tscn instances with north_wall.gd
  The rest are plain StaticBody2D wall tiles — indestructible
  A rusty metal gate at tile (18,12) — locked, decorative
  Interact with gate: "[Welded shut. Has been for thirty years.]"

ZONE B — Kazakh Side, Northern Approach (rows 14-24)
  Slightly different tile palette — Kazakh side is marginally
  better maintained (hint that the North Aral Sea is recovering)
  Sparse dry grass tiles instead of pure sand
  A small concrete building tile (28,16) — supply depot
    Interior: shelves, equipment, the TNT pickup
    TNT Pickup at tile (29,17):
      Area2D with pickup_item.gd
      item_name: "tnt"  amount: 1  auto_equip: false
    Interact with shelf: "[Military surplus. Most of it expired.]"
    "[The TNT however looks functional. Unfortunately.]"
  Guard patrol path: along wall from tile (4,14) to (32,14)
    Patrol loop: 28 tiles east-west, 12 second cycle
  A Kazakh flag on a short pole tile (5,16) — decorative
  Water level marker (same as Muynak) tile (32,20)
    Interact: "[Kazakhstan side. The North Aral Sea water level.]"
              "[Last entry: +4.2 meters. Rising since 2005.]"
              "[It is working on their side. It can work here too.]"

ZONE C — Post-Explosion (rows 0-28, triggered state)
  After TNT detonates, the 6 NorthWall tiles are replaced
  with rubble sprites (handled in north_wall.gd queue_free)
  Rising water node activates at tile (18,13) — the gap
  Water rises from that gap outward, filling the screen

ZONE D — Border Guard Handshake Zone (tile 18,14)
  After dialogue completes and TNT is accepted,
  both Rustam and the guard walk to this center point
  Handshake animation triggers here (cutscene-only frames)
  This is a 2-second scripted moment — no player control

ATMOSPHERE NOTES
────────────────
The border level is the only level that feels geopolitical.
But it should not feel adversarial.
The guard is not an obstacle. He is an ally who cannot
officially help but will do everything unofficially.
The mines are real danger — but clearly telegraphed.
The wall itself is the antagonist. Not the people.

After the explosion: pure joy. The map should feel
completely different. Water rising. The wall gone.
The two men standing on either side of where it was.
This is the only moment in the game with no dialogue.
Just the sound of water and the two of them watching it come.
