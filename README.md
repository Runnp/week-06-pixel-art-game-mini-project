Rustam: Lost Waters of Aral is a pixel-art story game built in Godot 4, inspired by the real life of Rustam, a young man from Karakalpakstan, Uzbekistan, who dedicated himself to restoring the Aral Sea, once the fourth largest lake on Earth and now one of its greatest environmental disasters. You play through five chapters across a dying landscape: from a quiet hometown where grandmothers remember blue water, to the ghost ships of Muynak rusting in open desert, to the dry seabed itself where the magic of the land still flickers beneath the salt. Along the way you will plant trees, scrap ships, raise fish, face creatures born from the crisis, and ultimately travel back to 1953 Soviet Tashkent to speak with the bureaucrat who signed the river diversion order but not to stop him, that might change the whole Central Asia, but to ask him to add one footnote, one clause, one minimum flow of water that nobody enforced for decades but nobody ever formally repealed either. It is a game about restoration being slow, imperfect, and worth doing anyway.
